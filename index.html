Identity

Who I am

I work on systems that operate under real constraints, with a focus on control, structure, uncertainty, and how ideas hold up outside ideal conditions.

My work usually begins with a question about stability. I am interested in how systems behave when theory meets friction, noise, and imperfect environments.

I approach problems through simulation, iterative design, and system level reasoning, and I also use visual media as another way to observe structure and motion.

Thinking

Notes on Practice

Short principles that frame how I think about engineering, failure, and the relationship between systems and people.

Thinking 01

Engineering with Empathy

Systems should be designed with awareness of the people, environments, and limitations they will actually encounter.

Thinking 02

Beyond Ideal Conditions

A system that only works in clean assumptions is still incomplete. Robustness begins where certainty ends.

Thinking 03

Failure as Signal

Simulation and testing matter most when they reveal where a model stops holding, not when they simply confirm success.
